Walk down any shampoo aisle and you'll see the same words repeated everywhere: *moisturizing*, *strengthening*, *repair*, *salon-quality*. None of those words are regulated. None of them mean anything specific. What actually matters is what's listed after the word *Ingredients* — and most people have never been taught how to read it.

As a licensed cosmetologist, I look at ingredient lists the same way I look at a client's hair: what's really going on here, underneath the marketing?

## The Basics: How the List Works

Cosmetic ingredients in the U.S. are listed in descending order by concentration. The first ingredient is the most abundant. By the time you reach the bottom third of the list, you're often dealing with amounts under 1%.

This matters because a shampoo can legally advertise "with argan oil" even if argan oil appears as the 27th ingredient — after the fragrance, the preservatives, and the colorant. You're paying for the promise, not the product.

## Ingredients I Actually Want to See

**Water** as ingredient #1 is normal and expected — shampoo is a water-based formula.

**Sodium cocoyl isethionate, sodium lauroyl methyl isethionate, or cocamidopropyl betaine** — these are gentler surfactants (cleansing agents) derived from coconut. They lift oil and debris without stripping the hair's natural moisture barrier.

**Aloe vera, panthenol (vitamin B5), glycerin** — humectants that attract and hold moisture. These show up in well-formulated shampoos and actually benefit the hair shaft.

**Hydrolyzed proteins** (wheat, silk, keratin, rice) — when these appear in the first half of the list, they can temporarily fill gaps in the cuticle and add strength. If they're near the bottom, they're mostly decorative.

**Botanical extracts** — rosemary, chamomile, peppermint. These can support scalp health and stimulation. Look for them in the top 10–12 ingredients to actually matter.

## Ingredients Worth Avoiding (or at Least Understanding)

**Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)** — the most common shampoo surfactant and also the harshest. Effective at removing oil, but it can disrupt the scalp's moisture barrier with daily use. Fine for an occasional clarifying wash, but not ideal for every day — especially for color-treated or dry hair.

**Sodium laureth sulfate (SLES)** — a milder cousin of SLS, used in many mainstream shampoos. Gentler, but still worth noting if you have a sensitive scalp.

**Silicones** (look for anything ending in *-cone*, *-xane*, or *-siloxane*) — these coat the hair shaft and create the slip and shine you feel right out of the shower. Not inherently bad, but they build up over time and can prevent moisture from penetrating the hair. If you use a silicone-heavy shampoo and conditioner, plan for a monthly clarifying wash.

**Fragrance / parfum** — a single word that can represent dozens of undisclosed chemical compounds. For clients with sensitive scalps or skin conditions, this is often the culprit behind irritation.

**Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives** — DMDM hydantoin, quaternium-15, imidazolidinyl urea. These extend shelf life and are considered safe at low concentrations by U.S. standards, but they've been at the center of major consumer lawsuits. TRESemmé and OGX both faced class action suits alleging that DMDM hydantoin in their formulas caused significant hair loss. Both brands have since reformulated. If you see these preservatives on a label, it's worth knowing the history.

## What I Use at The Gilded Edge

After trying a lot of lines over the years, I landed on **Renpure** as my go-to shampoo at the salon — and my reasoning is practical.

I wash clients' hair all day. That means my hands are in water and product constantly. I chose Renpure partly because I can use it on my own skin while I work without irritation. If a formula is gentle enough that I'm not reaching for a hand cream every hour, it's gentle enough for a client's scalp.

Beyond that, I genuinely appreciate how Renpure formulates for scalp health. The botanical ingredients aren't just for the bottle — they show up early in the list, which tells me the concentration is real. And that aligns with how I think about hair in general:

**Healthy hair starts with a healthy scalp.** Shampoo isn't just about cleaning the hair shaft — it's the one product that contacts your scalp directly, every time. A formula that strips and irritates the scalp leads to dryness, flaking, and over-production of oil. A formula that supports the scalp's natural balance leads to healthier growth over time.

## The Shampoo-for-Every-Day Myth

Most people shampoo more often than they need to. Daily shampooing — especially with a sulfate-heavy formula — can strip the scalp's natural sebum, which triggers *more* oil production as compensation. The scalp overproduces to compensate, and suddenly you feel like you *have* to wash every day.

If you're in that cycle, try spacing washes out by one day and using a dry shampoo at the roots in between. Most scalps will recalibrate within two to three weeks.

## The Short Version

- Read the ingredient list, not the front label
- Gentler surfactants (isethionates, betaines) > harsh sulfates for everyday use
- Beneficial ingredients need to appear in the first half of the list to matter
- Silicones aren't the enemy, but budget for a monthly clarifying wash
- Scalp health = hair health — your shampoo is the foundation
